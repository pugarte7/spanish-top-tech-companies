# spanish-top-tech-companies

What companies in Spain pay software engineers with 5+ years of experience on
average, counting only salaries of 60k+ base. Read
[docs/CONTEXT.md](docs/CONTEXT.md) before changing anything in the data
pipeline — it records why several obvious-looking approaches are wrong.

**One average per company, of qualifying salaries only.** `companies.csv`
holds individual salaries: each row one software engineer in Spain with 5 or
more years of experience (`lib.SENIOR_YEARS`) and a base of 60k or more
(`lib.THRESHOLD_EUR`). The README shows one row per company, the mean of those
salaries and how many there are. Seniority is the years, never the level name:
an L4 with eight years counts, a "Senior" with two does not. No other job
families, and nothing else in the mean: not an all-levels median, not a ladder
mean, not engineers under 5 years or under 60k. The maintainer set this on
2026-09-16, after rejecting averages across levels, a filter on titles that
said senior, and a mean over every 5+ year engineer including those under 60k;
do not bring any of them back.

**The README is the product.** Every company, its average and every link
belongs in `README.md`, generated by `scripts/build.py`. `companies.csv` is the
only data file: one row per salary, company columns repeated. Never slim the
README by moving data into side files, and never go back to one file per
company. Both were tried and the maintainer rejected them.

The four things that will bite you:

1. **Levels.fyi serves another country's salaries when it has no Spanish data**,
   and the page currency stays EUR for Germany, the Netherlands, France and
   Ireland. Guard on each submission's own location, never on currency and
   never on `locationMeta`. `scripts/fetch_spain.py` is the only script that
   writes pay; `scripts/fetch_company.py` is details-only for exactly this
   reason. `validate.py` rejects any levels.fyi source URL without
   `/locations/` in it, or that is not a company's software-engineer page,
   which is the backstop that catches whatever a fetcher gets wrong — leave it
   in place. This trap has been paid for twice: 682 foreign bands, then 205
   more carried in by a branch cut before the first fix.
2. **Submissions live in two places on the per-location page.** `averages`
   carries up to twenty `samples` per level, each one engineer with a city,
   years of experience and pay; `median` is one more, and the only one many
   small companies have. It is often also a sample, so dedupe on `uuid`.
   `percentiles` is an all-levels aggregate and is never an entry. A level's
   `count` is usually higher than its samples: the page does not publish them
   all.
3. **`sampleSize > 0` does not mean anything is published.** Many companies
   report a count with every percentile null and no samples, and
   `generatedOccupationSchema.sampleSize` is the company's *global* count
   whenever `averages` is empty.
4. **Every figure in the payload is USD.** Multiply by `locationExchangeRate`.
   The median record's own `exchangeRate` converts to the currency its author
   was paid in, so use it only when `baseSalaryCurrency` is EUR. Missing the
   conversion once put all 141 bands on the front page 16% high; using the
   record's rate for a dollar salary listed Smile.io's 105k USD as 105k EUR.

All Levels.fyi fetching must run from Spain; the pages are IP-scoped. Keep
`--delay` at 2.5s or more, and treat 403/405/429/503 as throttling rather than
as "not found".

Entries are individual Levels.fyi submissions. Keep the stored fields to what
the README shows (no submission ids, exact days, titles or years at company),
keep the attribution, and remove anything someone asks to have removed.

A row should ideally mean the maintainer knows someone in Spain doing that job,
or was offered the position. Crowdsourced figures are placeholders; first-hand
entries (`community`, `offer-letter`) sort above them.

A company with no qualifying salary still gets a row. The `spain_check_*`
columns record when Levels.fyi was asked and what its Spain page had instead,
and the README renders that rather than a dash, so "asked, nothing published"
never reads as "nobody has looked". Never infer that state from the wording of
a note.

After any data change: `python3 scripts/validate.py && python3 scripts/build.py`,
and commit the regenerated `README.md` and `companies.csv` — CI fails otherwise.
